\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Unless stated otherwise, numbers are held-out $\Rsq$ on the fixed 311-example
test split, and the reconstruction gaps carry a paired bootstrap 95\% confidence
interval over test examples (10{,}000 resamples; \cref{app:stats}). Two
exceptions, flagged where they occur: the depth series
(\cref{sec:evaluation:depth}) reports mean$\pm$sd over three refits rather than
a bootstrap CI, and the causal group effects (\cref{sec:evaluation:causal})
bootstrap over the eight candidate features, which are the independent unit
there. Several supporting numbers --- the T1--T5 battery, the sublayer split,
the Qwen full-depth contrast --- carry no interval at all. Model
selection performed on the test split is limited to points where the contrast
itself is the pre-registered quantity; the one CI where selection could matter
(SSM vs CLT) is re-derived with validation-based selection in
\cref{sec:evaluation:clt}. \Cref{app:operating} tabulates every operating
point and which claim uses which.

\subsection{Headline: cross-layer state beats a matched per-layer dictionary}
\label{sec:evaluation:gate3}

Under the LASSO protocol, the SSM sits above its state-ablated twin at every
sparsity level of the $\lambda_{L1}$ sweep (\cref{fig:pareto}): held-out
$\Rsq$ 0.867--0.698 versus 0.816--0.653 across $\lambda_{L1} \in
\{0.3,\dots,30\}$. At the canonical $\lambda_{L1}{=}1$ knee the gap is
\begin{gather*}
  \textbf{+0.0489}\; \CI{+0.0464}{+0.0516} \text{ (MLSAE source)},\\
  \textbf{+0.0483}\; \CI{+0.0458}{+0.0509} \text{ (trained-L1 source)},
\end{gather*}
--- dictionary-independent, seed-robust (three seeds give
$+0.0504 \pm 0.0014$; the CIs above are seed 0, the recorded canonical fit),
and it survives an object-disjoint split
($+0.055$/$+0.061$ matched-$L_0$ at the two central sparsities). At the knee
the two arms are not exactly matched in sparsity (SSM $L_0$ 0.0149 vs baseline
0.0133 as a fraction of alive atoms); interpolating the baseline Pareto to the
SSM's $L_0$ moves the gap to $+0.048$, so the mismatch is not doing the work.
The same interpolation across the full sweep spans $+0.037$ to $+0.066$, but
its maximum is upward-biased (it pairs against a baseline tail that has
saturated in $L_0$), which is why the knee gap, not that range, is the
statistic we carry forward (\cref{app:prereg}, deviation 5).

\paragraph{Matched parameters, not just matched rank.}
The gap above compares arms at equal rank, giving the SSM roughly twice the
baseline's parameters (8.36M vs 4.22M). Widening the baseline to rank 64
(8.41M) closes most of it: under the LASSO protocol the state-path edge
survives at \textbf{$+0.0077$ \CI{$+0.0053$}{$+0.0102$}} --- small, real, and
conservative (the wider baseline runs slightly denser) --- while under the
frozen-source protocol the same comparison is a wash ($\pm 0.005$, opposite
signs at the 8.4M and 16.8M budgets). We therefore quote the headline as
``\textbf{$+0.049$ rank-matched, $+0.008$ param-matched}'' and never write
``load-bearing'' without the second number. What decoder width cannot
replicate at any budget is the \emph{structure} the state provides ---
\cref{sec:evaluation:operator,sec:evaluation:probe}.

\subsection{The gain grows with depth}
\label{sec:evaluation:depth}

Repeating the fit at every depth in the model zoo and reporting the
\emph{knee-gap} statistic (the raw same-$\lambda$ gap at the $\lambda_{L1}=1$
knee; the earlier max-over-$\lambda$ statistic is upward-biased and
seed-fragile because the baseline Pareto's tail saturates in $L_0$ --- both
statistics are archived) gives a monotone in-family depth series with tight
3-seed error bars:
\[
  \text{Pythia: } 6L\;\; {+0.0076 \pm 0.0012} \;\to\;
  12L\;\; {+0.0130 \pm 0.0019} \;\to\;
  24L\;\; {+0.0504 \pm 0.0014},
\]
with cross-family 12-layer points GPT-2-small $+0.0175 \pm 0.0025$ and Qwen2
$+0.0413 \pm 0.0025$ (\cref{fig:depthscaling}). These are single points, one
per family: they replicate the gain's \emph{sign}, not its growth with depth,
which is a within-Pythia statement from three points.

Five caveats are part of this figure's claim, and together they are the reason
it is offered as suggestive rather than settled. (i) The Pythia line mixes two
TopK suites (EleutherAI-32k at 70m, MLSAE elsewhere), and the cross-family
points use L1 suites, so dictionary type is not held fixed anywhere in the
figure. (ii) Within a family, depth is
confounded with width and parameter count (deconfounding via Pythia-1b/1.4b is
recorded future work). (iii) Training-set size also varies along the Pythia
line ($N = 1{,}000 \to 767 \to 1{,}555$ at 6/12/24 layers), and
\cref{sec:evaluation:clt} shows cross-layer advantage is strongly $N$-dependent
in exactly this range, so $N$ is a live confound for the depth trend.
(iv) The 6-layer point is the Pythia-70m debug lane, whose dataset is built
differently (top-1{,}000 candidates by confidence rather than
threshold-filtered; \cref{app:crossmodel}). (v) The plotted Qwen point is the
12-layer \emph{SAE-coverage prefix of a 24-layer model} --- a
dictionary-controlled contrast (our own L1 SAEs on the same rows at 24 vs 12
layers) gives \textbf{$+0.072$ at full depth vs $+0.043$ on the prefix}, so
public-suite coverage, not depth, limits the plotted point. That $+0.072$ is
an L1-sourced number and \pythia{}'s $+0.050$ is TopK-sourced, so we do not
read the two against each other.

\paragraph{How much of the gain is learned?}
Running the identical pipeline on a \emph{randomly initialised} \pythia{}
(sources retrained on its own activations) yields a positive but smaller gap:
mean over the $\lambda$ sweep \textbf{$+0.025$ untrained vs $+0.046$ trained}
--- the untrained network carries \textbf{53\%} of the trained gain
(per-$\lambda$ shares averaged), and the
learned share \emph{grows with sparsity} (66\% learned at
$\lambda_{L1}{=}10$). The split replicates on GPT-2-small (untrained carries
44\%). Read against \cref{sec:phase1:pressure}, this is a dissociation
\emph{on the primary model only}: there, AR-on-$\Delta$ \emph{predictability}
is architectural (the untrained network \emph{exceeds} the trained one once
persistence is stripped) while the SSM \emph{reconstruction win} keeps a
$\approx$half learned component --- so the decomposition sees something
training adds that the raw AR statistic does not, concentrated at the sparse
operating points interpretability cares about. On GPT-2-small the two
statistics agree instead (AR learned share $47\%$, reconstruction learned
share $56\%$), so the dissociation is a one-family observation and we do not
claim it more broadly. What carries across both families is the reconstruction
result itself: roughly half the gain is learned.

\subsection{Baselines that were not built for
\texorpdfstring{$\Delta$}{Delta}, and it shows}
\label{sec:evaluation:baselines}

The pre-registered indirect baselines both score \emph{below zero} on
update reconstruction: differencing the pretrained per-layer SAE's state
reconstructions ($\hat\Delta_\ell = \hat v_{\ell+1} - \hat v_\ell$) gives
$\Rsq = \mathbf{-0.223}$ even though the same SAE reconstructs the
\emph{states} at $+0.60$ --- the two reconstruction-error terms
$\epsilon_{\ell+1}-\epsilon_\ell$ swamp the small update signal, the bias the
protocol predicted, dramatic at 24 layers --- and the Tuned-Lens-implied
update gives $\mathbf{-0.309}$ (pooled; the layer-averaged version of the same
baseline is the $-0.691$ of \cref{sec:phase1:ridge}). The two
$\Delta$-targeted methods (SSM
$+0.867$, matched per-layer dictionary $+0.816$, both at the sweep's densest
point $\lambda_{L1}{=}0.3$) against the two indirect
ones is the clearest statement of why the update needs to be modelled
directly.

\subsection{Against the unconstrained \texorpdfstring{$O(L^2)$}{O(L2)}
parameterisation (CLT)}
\label{sec:evaluation:clt}

The primary conceptual comparison fits a \emph{residual-write CLT} --- a free
low-rank decoder $W^{\ell'\to\ell}$ at every (source,\,target) pair,
$\hat\Delta_\ell = b_\ell + \sum_{\ell' \le \ell} W^{\ell'\to\ell} u_{\ell'}$
with the same unpenalised per-layer bias the SSM gets, the
unconstrained family our SSM restricts --- on the same frozen sources at the
same per-decoder rank, under the frozen-source protocol. Scope qualifiers
stated once and inherited by every sentence below: residual-write variant (not
MLP-write), frozen shared sources (CLT does not get its own dictionary),
per-pair rank capped at 32--64 (rank 128 exceeds the study GPU;
\cref{app:repro}), $N = 1{,}244$ training examples (1{,}057 where a validation
carve-out is used), and no natively-trained
CLT (limitation). The honest summary is three axes (\cref{fig:clt}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Matched per-decoder rank: a small, real CLT edge.} At rank 32
    the best-of-grid gap is SSM$-$CLT $= -0.0142$ \CI{$-0.0185$}{$-0.0099$};
    re-selecting $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$ on a train-internal validation split
    (never test) reproduces it at \textbf{$-0.0090$ \CI{$-0.0136$}{$-0.0045$}}.
    The edge exists at every depth tested --- at the shared grid
    $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}{=}1$ used for all models: 6L $-0.009$; 12L
    $-0.050$ GPT-2, $-0.023$ Pythia-160m; 24L $-0.011$; Qwen2's coarse-grid
    point, $-0.021$, agrees in sign but carries no CI --- and is \emph{not}
    monotone in depth; it tracks model and dictionary. (An earlier apparent tie
    was an artefact of a too-short regularisation grid; we report the corrected
    numbers. Widening the grid moves the primary model's CLT from $0.804$ to
    $0.812$, which is why the depth list above and axis 1's headline differ.)
  \item \textbf{Matched total parameters: the SSM Pareto-dominates up to
    52M.} Comparing each family at its best grid point, SSM rank-64 (16.9M
    parameters, $\Rsq\,0.8155$) beats CLT rank-16 (26.2M, 0.794) \emph{and}
    CLT rank-32 (52.4M, 0.8117); the CLT needs 105M (rank 64, 0.843) to lead
    outright. The rank-32 margin is thin ($+0.004$) and, being a cross-run
    comparison, carries no paired CI --- what the axis supports is that the
    SSM matches or beats the CLT at ${\sim}3\times$ fewer parameters, not that
    it beats it by a lot. Per-parameter, the first-order state is the better
    spend in this regime.
  \item \textbf{Sample efficiency: the CLT edge is data-bought.} Subsampling
    the train set flips the sign: at $N{=}300$ the SSM leads by $+0.0204$
    \CI{$+0.0163$}{$+0.0248$}, at $N{=}600$ by $+0.0128$, at $N{=}1{,}244$
    the CLT leads by $0.0105$ (the $N$-series holds one shared regularisation
    grid fixed at every $N$; the extended-grid and validation-selected
    full-$N$ gaps are axis 1's $-0.0142$/$-0.0090$) --- crossover at roughly
    $10^3$ examples for
    52M free parameters versus 8.4M shared ones.
\end{enumerate}

Both cross-layer methods beat the no-propagation per-layer decoder at every
depth tested (SSM$-$per-layer $+0.022$ to $+0.052$; CLT$-$per-layer $+0.045$
to $+0.072$; on the primary model
SSM$-$per-layer $+0.0518$ \CI{$+0.0485$}{$+0.0552$} under validation-based
selection). Read together
with H2's failure, the conclusion is symmetric and, we think, useful in both
directions: the free per-pair decoders are \emph{validated} --- they buy a
small, significant, data-dependent margin, so nothing forces a shared kernel
--- and the first-order state-space compression captures most of the
cross-layer reconstruction value at ${\sim}16\%$ of the parameters, dominating
at matched budget and in low data.

\subsection{Stability: the dictionary, not the model, controls identifiability}
\label{sec:evaluation:stability}

Gate 3 recorded one failed clause: top-100 decoder-direction stability across
re-initialisations was 0.45 (match@0.7), below the pre-registered 0.7, while
reconstruction was seed-identical --- an equivalent-minima degeneracy, unmoved
by $100\times$ stronger operator regularisation and by shrinking $d_z$. Three
findings close the story (\cref{fig:stability}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It is the TopK sparsity mechanism.} Training matched L1 and
    TopK dictionaries on the \emph{same} cached activations --- identical
    architecture, matched $L_0$, differing only in the sparsity operator ---
    and refitting: decoder match@0.7 --- the fraction of the top-100 features
    whose decoder direction has cross-seed cosine $\ge 0.7$ --- is
    \textbf{1.00 (L1)} versus \textbf{0.08
    (trained TopK)} versus 0.45 (pretrained MLSAE TopK), at the same
    reconstruction win ($+0.041$--$+0.047$). Not a width effect: the two
    trained dictionaries have identical nominal width (8{,}192) and it is the
    \emph{stable} one whose alive set is larger (1{,}178 L1 vs 660 TopK), so
    stability is not bought by narrowness. Cross-model, L1 sources are stable on two further
    families (GPT-2-small 0.95, Qwen2 0.81) while TopK is unstable on two
    Pythia models (0.38, 0.45), with $N$ ruled out by subsampling; the zoo's
    one \emph{stable} TopK point is the six-layer debug lane's EleutherAI
    $k{=}16$ suite (0.99, \cref{app:crossmodel}), so the failure is not
    TopK-universal --- but every TopK source we tested at $\ge$12 layers was
    unstable and every L1 source stable. Retraining
    the L1 dictionary on the train split only (no test leakage) changes
    nothing (match 1.00; gaps $+0.037$--$+0.070$).
  \item \textbf{It is not a gauge artefact.} Gauge-invariant per-feature
    impulse responses (feedthrough plus downstream state path) are as unstable
    (0.29) as raw decoder columns (0.36 over the gauge analysis's 200-feature
    candidate set; the Gate-3 top-100 statistic quoted above is 0.45), so the
    instability is genuine
    single-feature non-identifiability over redundant TopK atoms --- expected
    for a distributed code where the fit only constrains realised
    combinations.
  \item \textbf{Everything the claims use is stable.} Reconstruction (0.880
    every seed), the state-operator spectrum (median relative difference of
    the sorted moduli 0.086 across seeds --- \emph{not} the sorted-modulus
    cosine of 0.999, which random spectra also achieve;
    \cref{sec:evaluation:operator}), the gauge-invariant state-path decay
    (gap-2 ratio 0.42--0.49 across seeds and dictionaries), the top-feature
    decoder \emph{subspace} ($\sim$0.6 overlap), and
    the feature \emph{set} across data halves (Jaccard 0.89).
\end{enumerate}

We flag the first finding as independently useful: pipelines that interpret
\emph{individual} SAE features while sourcing from TopK dictionaries are
building on directions that need not survive a re-seed.

\subsection{The causal existence proof: a clean, fully controlled null}
\label{sec:evaluation:causal}

The pre-registered critical test: find a seed-stable, interpretable feature,
subtract its estimated contribution $\delta_\ell$ from the residual stream
during the real forward pass (re-solving the LASSO with the frozen decoder,
so reconstruction error stays untouched), and require --- all four
pre-registered --- a $\ge 0.20$ mean top-1 probability drop on the feature's
top-100 examples, preserved controls, a small matched-random-feature effect,
and a symmetric $\ge 1.0$-nat injection effect on 70\% of silent examples.

\textbf{The test fails, cleanly.} Across the six strongest candidates (from a
pool of 53 seed-stable features with purity 1.00 and legible themes), the
largest ablation effect is \textbf{$+0.024$} --- roughly an eighth of the
0.20 threshold ---
and the injection test returns $-0.02$ nats; controls and magnitude-matched
random features are $\approx 0$, so the splice mechanism is clean. This is
the pre-registered honest landing place, and we report it as such.

The diagnostics then locate \emph{why}, with an escalating control sequence
(\cref{fig:distributed}): amplifying one feature's $\delta_\ell$ ten-fold
drives the answer probability most of the way to zero, saturating at full
removal by $30\times$ (direction correct, natural magnitude small); ablating
each example's top-$k$ \emph{co-firing} features --- pooled over eight
seed-stable candidates, a superset of the six screened above --- crosses
threshold at $k{=}100$ ($+0.237$, CI \CI{0.213}{0.262}, bootstrapped over the
eight candidates, which are the independent unit here, not over test
examples); a
\emph{count-matched} random group does not ($+0.034$) --- but a
\emph{mass-matched} random group reproduces it ($+0.244$, i.e.\ slightly
\emph{exceeding} the real group), and, decisively, a
\textbf{norm-matched random direction} --- even an isotropic one in
$\mathbb{R}^D$ --- reproduces it too ($+0.224$ isotropic, $+0.204$
contribution-shaped, crossing at the same $k$). Against the isotropic
direction, feature identity retains a significant but ${\sim}5\%$ sliver
($+0.012$ \CI{$+0.004$}{$+0.021$}); against the contribution-shaped direction
$+0.033$ \CI{$+0.020$}{$+0.047$}; against the mass-matched \emph{feature}
group, $-0.008$. We quote the isotropic contrast because it is the control
that isolates direction alone, and note that no control leaves feature
identity with more than a seventh of the effect.
Off-target specificity is weak and class-dependent (pooled on-target $+2.89$
vs off-target $+2.45$ nats; geography features answer-specific, organization
features not). The group effect is therefore \emph{magnitude-driven}: it
demonstrates the query position's generic sensitivity to perturbations of
that size, not a causally privileged code.

Swapping to the clean L1 dictionary strengthens rather than rescues the null:
the candidate pool becomes 199/200 seed-stable, the maximum single-feature
effect rises nearly five-fold to $+0.112$ --- a less redundant dictionary
\emph{concentrates} causal mass --- and still no feature approaches 0.20.
\textbf{Factual recall in this model is a distributed, mass-driven code with
no single-feature bottleneck, independent of the dictionary used to read it.}
No threshold was adjusted post hoc.

\subsection{The state is functionally informative, not reconstruction-only}
\label{sec:evaluation:probe}

Does the 32-dimensional state $z_\ell$ carry task information, or is it only
reconstruction machinery? We probe four arms at matched dimension
$d{=}32$ with identical linear probes (hyperparameters selected on a
train-internal validation carve-out): the state; PCA-32 of the current
layer's code $u_\ell$; PCA-32 of the concatenated prefix codes $u_{\le\ell}$
(an information superset of what the state sees); and PCA-32 of the raw
residual $\vl$ as a reference. Target: 205-way answer identity (majority
0.106; permuted-label null at majority).

At mid-depth the state wins against both code summaries on both dictionaries
(\cref{fig:probe}): state$-$code $+0.060$ \CI{$+0.037$}{$+0.084$} (MLSAE) /
$+0.139$ \CI{$+0.105$}{$+0.173$} (L1); state$-$prefix $+0.080$/$+0.173$ at
mid-depth and $+0.110$/$+0.188$ late; at $\ell{=}17$ --- the
answer-promotion depth where $\kstar$ blips (\cref{fig:kstar}) and per-layer
reconstruction dips (\cref{fig:prefix}, right) --- the state jumps from
$0.57$ to $0.76$, a full layer before the code and raw-residual arms make
their own jump, and beats even the raw-residual PCA there ($0.759$ vs
$0.666$). Peak state decoding
reaches 0.775/0.801 top-1 from 32 dimensions (majority class 0.106; with 205
classes over 311 test examples, top-1 is dominated by the frequent objects,
so we read the \emph{contrasts between matched arms}, not the absolute level).
Since the SSM is linear, the
state and the prefix-PCA are two 32-dimensional \emph{linear images of the
same prefix codes}: the compression trained only to reconstruct $\Delta$
retains answer-identity information that the variance-optimal compression
discards. Scope, stated plainly: the late band washes out against a per-layer
code summary, the raw stream remains the best readout late
($-0.04$ to $-0.07$), relation class saturates for every arm, and model
confidence favours the code arms --- the state's advantage is specific to
\emph{what} is being recalled, at the depths where recall is assembled.

\subsection{The learned state path attenuates fast}
\label{sec:evaluation:operator}

The state operators are the decomposition's structural readout. Getting the
epistemics right here requires a gauge argument we initially got wrong, so we
state it first.

\paragraph{What is and is not a property of the fit.}
\Cref{eq:ssm} has \emph{free per-layer} $A_\ell, B_\ell, C_\ell$. Its
symmetry group is therefore a \emph{per-layer} $T_\ell \in GL(d_z)$,
\[
  A_\ell \mapsto T_{\ell+1} A_\ell T_\ell^{-1}, \quad
  B_\ell \mapsto T_{\ell+1} B_\ell, \quad
  C_\ell \mapsto C_\ell T_\ell^{-1}, \quad
  z_0 \mapsto T_0 z_0,
\]
which leaves every $\hat\Delta_\ell$ unchanged. Only a \emph{global}
$T_\ell \equiv T$ acts on $A_\ell$ by similarity, so \textbf{the eigenvalues of
$A_\ell$ are not gauge-invariant}: even the scalar subgroup $T_\ell = c_\ell I$
rescales $\rho(A_\ell)$ by $c_{\ell+1}/c_\ell$. Applying a random
output-preserving scalar gauge to our fitted checkpoint (predictions unchanged
to $2\times10^{-7}$) moves the median $\rho$ from $0.43$ to $0.26$, the maximum
from $0.88$ to $4.91$, and the count of non-contractive transitions from
$0/23$ to $5/23$. The quantity that \emph{is} invariant --- the $T_\ell$
telescope out of it --- is the realised state path
$C_\ell\,(A_{\ell-1}\cdots A_{j+1})\,B_j$ from source layer $j$ to update
$\ell$. Under the same gauge it is unchanged to $1\times10^{-8}$.

\paragraph{The invariant result.}
The state-path kernel norm \textbf{halves per layer of depth gap}: normalised
to gap 1, $1 \to \mathbf{0.485} \to 0.211 \to 0.089$ across gaps $1..4$, an
$e$-folding memory of $\tau = \mathbf{1.15}$ layers ($\tau$ is the
least-squares log-linear fit over gaps 1--8; the per-gap ratio tightens from
$0.49$ at gap 2 to ${\sim}0.40$--$0.44$ beyond gap 4, which is why the fitted
$\tau$ sits slightly below the $1/\ln 2 \approx 1.44$ that exact halving would
give). This is stable across
three seeds and both dictionaries (gap-2 ratio $0.42$--$0.49$, $\tau =
1.15$--$1.21$ layers). A source two layers back contributes half what one layer
back does, three back a fifth --- so first-order reads capture essentially all
state value, and this is a \emph{mechanism consistent with}
$\kstar\!\approx\!1$. The epistemic caveat still holds: it is estimated on the
same data on which $\kstar$ was measured, so it is an explanation within the
model class, not an independent confirmation; the model-free evidence for
$\kstar\!\approx\!1$ remains the per-depth AR sweeps of
\cref{sec:phase2:kstar}.

\paragraph{The gauge-dependent result, reported as such.}
In the minimum-Frobenius representative that the $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$
penalty selects, the operator is contractive at every layer (spectral radius
median $0.43$, maximum $0.88$, $0/23$ transitions $\ge 1$; the fit uses
$A_0,\dots,A_{22}$, since the transition out of the last state would feed no
update). That is a reproducible property of \emph{the regularised fit} --- it
recurs across seeds and dictionaries --- and we report it because it is what
one would look at, but no claim in this paper rests on it. We also retire one
statistic we previously quoted: the cross-seed cosine of the sorted modulus
spectrum is $0.999$, but \emph{two independent random spectra score $0.999$ on
the same statistic}, because both vectors are sorted and non-negative. The
honest cross-seed number is the median relative difference of the sorted
moduli, $\mathbf{0.086}$ (and $0.111$ between dictionaries); the per-layer
$\rho$ \emph{profile} correlates only $0.54$ across dictionaries. So the
modulus distribution is reproducible to about $10\%$ and the layer assignment
much less so.

Depth-varying (H2 fails) $+$ a state path that halves per layer $+$ a
${\sim}1$-layer memory is a compact, mechanistic summary of how these updates
travel: \emph{information written into the stream persists via the stream
itself; the incremental dynamics forget almost immediately.}

\subsection{Where the reconstruction lives: sources at every layer}
\label{sec:evaluation:prefix}

Keeping sources only up to layer $\ell_{\max}$, refitting, and letting the SSM
propagate them forward: held-out $\Rsq$ climbs monotonically over the sampled
cutoffs $\ell_{\max} \in \{0,3,7,11,15,19,23\}$, from $-0.44$
(embedding-only) through
$+0.20$ ($\ell_{\max}{=}11$) and $+0.58$ ($\ell_{\max}{=}19$) to $+0.77$
(full), still gaining $+0.19$ over the last four layers
(\cref{fig:prefix}, left). The embedding-only floor is degenerate by
construction --- $v_0$ has no SAE, so $u_0 \equiv 0$ and the state trajectory
is example-independent --- and its $-0.44$ is almost entirely one layer: the
per-layer $\Rsq$ is ${\approx}0$ at 23 of 24 depths and $-2.4$ at the
final write, whose norm is 2--3$\times$ any other's. Late-layer sources carry
reconstruction value that
propagation \emph{cannot} substitute --- the features are genuinely new at
depth, matching the staged-recall picture of \citet{geva2023dissecting} ---
which justifies the design's per-layer sources and completes a two-sided
ablation ladder with the state ablation: removing propagation costs the
headline-sized margin ($0.77 \to 0.73$ under this protocol --- the state
ablation of \cref{sec:evaluation:gate3} seen from the other side); removing
per-layer sources is catastrophic.
