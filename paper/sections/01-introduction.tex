\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Mechanistic interpretability decomposes transformer activations mostly layer by
layer. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) recover human-legible features from a single
layer's residual stream \citep{cunningham2023sparse,bricken2023monosemanticity,
templeton2024scaling}, but a per-layer dictionary answers only a per-layer
question --- \emph{what features live here?} --- and not the question that
matters for circuit analysis and causal editing: \emph{how does what a layer
writes relate to what earlier layers wrote?} The field has begun to attack
this directly. Crosscoders fit one dictionary across all layers
\citep{lindsey2024crosscoders}; multi-layer SAEs (MLSAEs) find that the same
latent fires at different depths on different prompts \citep{lawson2024mlsae};
and cross-layer transcoders (CLTs), the substrate of attribution-graph circuit
tracing, give every feature a \emph{free} decoder at every downstream layer ---
$O(L^2)$ independent decoder matrices with no prior connecting them
\citep{ameisen2025circuit}.

This paper asks whether that free-parameter budget is load-bearing or
redundant, and it asks the question the way one asks it in signal processing:
by modelling the \emph{increments}. Because the residual stream is additive,
$v_{\ell+1} = \vl + \Dl$, regressing states on states trivially recovers the
identity; the non-trivial object is the update $\Dl = v_{\ell+1} - \vl$ ---
what the layer \emph{adds} --- exactly as differencing removes the trend in
classical time-series analysis. We pre-registered two nested hypotheses about
these updates on factual-recall inputs:

\begin{description}
  \item[H1 (depth-local predictability).] $\Dl$ is predictable from the
    preceding updates $\Delta_{\ell-1}, \Delta_{\ell-2}, \dots$ by a low-rank
    linear map, above scrambled-activation and context-perturbation nulls ---
    residual updates form an autoregressive process along depth.
  \item[H2 (approximate stationarity).] The prediction kernel is approximately
    shared across depth, so that a single block-Toeplitz (convolutive) kernel
    $\Dl \approx \sum_k W_k\, s_{\ell-k}$ over sparse per-layer sources is a
    useful generative model. Under H2, CLT's $O(L^2)$ decoder matrices would
    collapse to $O(K)$ shared kernel taps.
\end{description}

The hypothesis was motivated by an analogy to convolutive blind source
separation in high-density surface electromyography, where sources mix into
recordings through characteristic propagation kernels
\citep{holobar2007multichannel}; the analogy generated the hypothesis and
nothing else --- the formal models and tests are native to interpretability
and classical statistics. Crucially, the study was \emph{pre-registered}: a
frozen protocol fixed the diagnostics, the go/no-go gates, the null controls,
the decomposition architecture as a function of the gate outcomes, and the
causal-evaluation thresholds, before any data were collected. Where the data
contradicted the more exciting branch, we took the pre-registered exit. Both
hypotheses could have failed cheaply; what actually happened is more
interesting than either clean outcome. Scope, stated up front: all kernels
are fit at the \emph{query position} of factual-recall prompts drawn from
three CounterFact relation classes. The binary characterisation replicates on
generic text, but cross-position and cross-class kernel transfer are both
negative (\cref{sec:phase2:scope}) --- the kernels are hyper-local to their
circuit, itself a finding --- so every claim below is a claim about that
regime.

\paragraph{What we found.} H1 survives every control we could construct ---
including two nulls specified in advance and a post-hoc pressure battery
designed to break it --- but decomposes on inspection: an exact
(Pythagorean) split of the headline autoregressive $\Rsq$ shows that on
factual prompts $0.65 = 0.52$ running-sum tautology $+\;0.13$ genuine
structure, and an untrained-network control shows the genuine part is
\emph{substantially architectural} --- a randomly initialised transformer
exhibits it at comparable strength, on two model families
(\cref{sec:phase1}). H2 fails decisively and non-negotiably: an AR kernel fit
on one third of the depth axis predicts another third's updates \emph{worse
than that band's own mean}, at every rank, with no clean segment boundary
(\cref{sec:phase2}). The pre-registered consequence is that the generative
model must be a first-order, fully depth-indexed state-space model (SSM) ---
and that CLT's refusal to share parameters across depth is \emph{validated},
not wasteful.

Fitting that SSM over frozen per-layer SAE sources yields the paper's central
positive result (\cref{sec:evaluation}): cross-layer state reconstructs
residual updates above a matched per-layer dictionary at every sparsity
($+0.049$ $\Rsq$ \CI{$+0.046$}{$+0.052$} at matched rank, $+0.008$
\CI{$+0.005$}{$+0.010$} at matched total parameters), the gain grows
monotonically with depth (6 layers $+0.008 \to$ 24 layers $+0.050$ within
Pythia; replicated in GPT-2 and Qwen2), and about half of it is present at
random initialisation --- so the method captures a genuinely learned signal
roughly equal in size to the architectural one. The learned state-transition
operator is contractive everywhere (spectral radius median $0.43$, implied
memory $\approx 0.8$ layers), consistent with --- and supplying a candidate
mechanism for --- the observed first-order structure, and the state is
functionally informative: at matched dimension it decodes the recalled answer
at mid-depth better than PCA summaries of the codes it is built from.

To be direct about the framing: this is a \emph{characterisation} paper, not
a method paper. We do not advance the SSM as a better decomposition tool on
the strength of the $+0.008$ parameter-matched margin --- read alone, that
number says most of the rank-matched gap is parameter count. The case that
the cross-layer state captures real structure rests on what decoder width
cannot buy at any budget: the monotone growth of the gain with depth, its
roughly-half-learned split against untrained controls, the contractive
operator spectrum, and the state's functional informativeness for the
recalled answer. Those structural results, not the headline gap, carry the
paper's positive claims.

The pre-registered causal test --- find a feature no baseline finds, ablate
it, predict the behavioural consequence --- returns a null we consider one of
the paper's main contributions (\cref{sec:evaluation:causal}). No single
feature clears the pre-registered effect threshold; ablating each example's
top-100 co-firing features does clear it, but a \emph{norm-matched random
direction} reproduces that group effect, so it is a magnitude effect, not
evidence of a privileged code. The null is dictionary-independent: a cleaner
L1 dictionary concentrates single-feature effects five-fold and still finds no
bottleneck. Factual recall in this model is a distributed, mass-driven code.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A pre-registered, gate-structured characterisation of residual-update
    dynamics: H1 holds (with an exact accounting of how much is tautology,
    and evidence the remainder is substantially architectural); H2 fails
    (pervasive non-stationarity, $\kstar\!\approx\!1$, query-position-specific
    and circuit-specific kernels); both replicate across six models, three
    families, and on generic Pile text (\cref{sec:phase1,sec:phase2}).
  \item A first-order, fully depth-indexed SSM decomposition of $\Dl$ over
    sparse per-layer sources, with a reconstruction gain over matched
    per-layer baselines that is CI-significant, depth-graded, and
    approximately half learned / half architectural
    (\cref{sec:decomposition,sec:evaluation}).
  \item A three-axis comparison against the unconstrained $O(L^2)$
    residual-write CLT parameterisation: at matched per-decoder rank the CLT
    keeps a small significant edge; at matched \emph{total} parameters the SSM
    Pareto-dominates up to 52M parameters; below ${\sim}1$k training examples
    the SSM generalises better (\cref{sec:evaluation:clt}).
  \item A clean, fully controlled causal null: recall is distributed and
    magnitude-driven, with no privileged sparse subset --- established with
    matched-count, matched-mass, and norm-matched random-direction controls,
    and robust to the dictionary (\cref{sec:evaluation:causal}).
  \item Two structural positives that survive every robustness check: the
    contractive propagation operator (gauge-invariant spectrum, seed cosine
    $0.999$, dictionary cosine $0.993$), and the functional informativeness of
    the SSM state for answer identity at mid-depth
    (\cref{sec:evaluation:operator,sec:evaluation:probe}).
  \item A practical warning for the field: TopK dictionaries make
    single-feature decoder directions seed-unidentifiable (match@0.7 $=0.45$)
    where matched L1 dictionaries trained on the same activations are
    perfectly stable ($1.00$) --- an identifiability failure that lives in the
    sparsity mechanism, not the model or the decomposition
    (\cref{sec:evaluation:stability}).
\end{enumerate}

documents were frozen before data collection and every deviation is logged
