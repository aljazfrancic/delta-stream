\begin{abstract}
Transformer components communicate through an additive residual stream, so
the update a layer writes, $\Dl = v_{\ell+1} - \vl$, is the natural unit of
cross-layer computation. We pre-registered two hypotheses about these updates
on factual-recall inputs --- (H1) they are predictable across depth; (H2) the
prediction kernel is approximately depth-stationary --- and ran the frozen
protocol on \pythia{}, replicating on five further models from three families.
\textbf{(1)}~H1 holds far above pre-registered nulls but decomposes: an exact
orthogonal split of the held-out explained variance, taken at the 50 most
persistent state directions, attributes most of it to slow drift along those
directions (the running-sum ``tautology'') on the four deepest updates
($0.65 = 0.52 + 0.13$) and roughly half on the whole stack
(raw $0.52 = 0.27 + 0.25$), and on the primary model
what survives is substantially architectural --- an untrained network exceeds
it (though on GPT-2-small it is about half learned).
\textbf{(2)}~H2 fails decisively: kernels fit on one depth band predict
another band's updates worse than that band's mean, so the right abstraction
is a first-order ($\kstar\!\approx\!1$), fully depth-indexed state-space
model (SSM), which supports the choice, in cross-layer transcoders, not to
share decoders along depth. \textbf{(3)}~Fitted over sparse per-layer
sources, the SSM
reconstructs $\Dl$ above a matched per-layer dictionary ($+0.049$ $\Rsq$
rank-matched; at matched parameters, $+0.008$ when codes are re-inferred
against the update and nothing when they are held fixed), with a roughly
half-learned gain
that grows with depth under the first of those two protocols and not the
second; its gauge-invariant state path attenuates by half per layer
of depth gap (memory ${\approx}1.2$ layers, consistent with
$\kstar\!\approx\!1$) and the state decodes the recalled
answer at mid-depth better than dimension-matched PCA of its own codes.
\textbf{(4)}~The pre-registered causal test returns a null, which post-hoc
controls locate: no single feature's ablation clears threshold, and the group
effect that does is reproduced by a norm-matched \emph{random direction} ---
read through either dictionary we tried, recall is a distributed,
magnitude-driven code. As a by-product, on the primary model
the SSM's per-feature write directions are reproduced across refits over L1
dictionaries but not over TopK dictionaries trained on the same activations
(three dictionaries each); in our small-data regime the dictionaries
themselves reproduce across training seeds under neither mechanism. All gates were
frozen, in an internal registration, before data collection; every headline
reconstruction gap carries a
paired bootstrap confidence interval, and every figure regenerates from tracked
tables.
\end{abstract}
