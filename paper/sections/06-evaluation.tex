\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Unless stated otherwise, numbers are held-out $\Rsq$ on the fixed 311-example
test split, and the reconstruction gaps carry a paired bootstrap 95\% confidence
interval over test examples (10{,}000 resamples; \cref{app:stats}). Two
exceptions, flagged where they occur: the depth series
(\cref{sec:evaluation:depth}) reports mean$\pm$sd over three refits rather than
a bootstrap CI, and the causal group effects (\cref{sec:evaluation:causal})
bootstrap over the eight candidate features, which are the independent unit
there. Several supporting numbers --- the T1--T5 battery, the sublayer split,
the Qwen full-depth contrast --- carry no interval at all. Model
selection performed on the test split is limited to points where the contrast
itself is the pre-registered quantity; the one CI where selection could matter
(SSM vs CLT) is re-derived with validation-based selection in
\cref{sec:evaluation:clt}. \Cref{app:operating} tabulates every operating
point and which claim uses which. A ``seed'' with no further qualification
is the seed of an SSM fit over a fixed dictionary, so three seeds are three
refits; a dictionary's training seed is always named as such
(\cref{sec:evaluation:stability,app:dictseeds}).

\subsection{Headline: cross-layer state beats a matched per-layer dictionary}
\label{sec:evaluation:gate3}

Under the LASSO protocol, the SSM sits above its state-ablated twin at every
sparsity level of the $\lambda_{L1}$ sweep (\cref{fig:pareto}): held-out
$\Rsq$ 0.867--0.698 versus 0.816--0.653 across $\lambda_{L1} \in
\{0.3,\dots,30\}$. At the canonical $\lambda_{L1}{=}1$
knee\footnote{``Knee'' is the sweep's sparsity--reconstruction bend, visible
in \cref{fig:pareto}: relative to $\lambda_{L1}{=}0.3$, $\lambda_{L1}{=}1$
buys a ${\sim}3\times$ lower $L_0$ for $-0.010$ $\Rsq$, while beyond it $L_0$
saturates and $\Rsq$ only falls. The same bend appears on the Pythia-70m
debug lane, and $\lambda_{L1}{=}1$ was frozen as the canonical operating
point when the Phase-4 checkpoint was persisted --- before the knee gap
became a reported statistic --- so the operating point was not selected on
the gap it now reports.} the gap is
\begin{gather*}
  \textbf{+0.0489}\; \CI{+0.0464}{+0.0516} \text{ (MLSAE source)},\\
  \textbf{+0.0483}\; \CI{+0.0458}{+0.0509} \text{ (trained-L1 source)},
\end{gather*}
--- the same over these two dictionaries, though not over every one (the
trained-TopK dictionary gives $+0.038$, \cref{sec:evaluation:stability}, and
on GPT-2-small the choice of dictionary moves the gap eight-fold,
\cref{sec:evaluation:depth}), seed-robust (three seeds give
$+0.0504 \pm 0.0014$; the CIs above are seed 0, the recorded canonical fit),
and it survives an object-disjoint split
($+0.055$/$+0.061$ matched-$L_0$ at the two central sparsities). At the knee
the two arms are not exactly matched in sparsity (SSM $L_0$ 0.0149 vs baseline
0.0133 as a fraction of alive atoms); interpolating the baseline Pareto to the
SSM's $L_0$ moves the gap to $+0.048$, so the mismatch is not doing the work.
The same interpolation across the full sweep spans $+0.037$ to $+0.066$, but
its maximum is upward-biased (it pairs against a baseline tail that has
saturated in $L_0$), which is why the knee gap, not that range, is the
statistic we carry forward (\cref{app:prereg}, deviation 5).

\paragraph{Matched parameters, not just matched rank.}
The gap above compares arms at equal rank, giving the SSM roughly twice the
baseline's parameters (8.36M vs 4.22M). Widening the baseline to rank 64
(8.41M) closes most of it: under the LASSO protocol the state-path edge
survives at \textbf{$+0.0077$ \CI{$+0.0053$}{$+0.0102$}} --- small, and
conservative in that the wider baseline runs slightly denser --- while under
the frozen-source protocol the same comparison is a wash ($\pm 0.005$,
opposite signs at the 8.4M and 16.8M budgets). That interval resamples test
examples from one fit per arm, so post hoc we repeated the comparison, still
at the knee (\cref{app:checks}). Over three refits the margin is $+0.0077$,
$+0.0098$ and $+0.0090$ ($+0.0089 \pm 0.0011$), and over the trained L1
dictionary it is $+0.0055$ \CI{$+0.0030$}{$+0.0081$}. It belongs to the
trained 24-layer model. On the untrained network, over an L1 dictionary
trained on its own activations, the rank-32 SSM falls $0.025$ below the
rank-64 per-layer decoder (it leads the rank-32 one by $0.029$); on
Pythia-160m (12 layers) it falls below by $0.0088 \pm 0.0008$ over three
refits (rank-matched, $+0.012$ to $+0.015$). At matched parameters the margin
is therefore learned rather than architectural, and at two depths in one
family it is present at 24 layers and not at 12: consistent with the depth
trend of \cref{sec:evaluation:depth}, which two points cannot establish. We
quote the headline as ``\textbf{$+0.049$ rank-matched, $+0.008$
param-matched}'' and never write ``load-bearing'' without the second number.
What decoder width cannot replicate at any budget is the \emph{structure}
the state provides --- \cref{sec:evaluation:operator}.

\subsection{The knee gap grows with depth}
\label{sec:evaluation:depth}

Repeating the fit at every depth in the model zoo (a post-hoc series) and
reporting the \emph{knee-gap} statistic (the raw same-$\lambda$ gap at the $\lambda_{L1}=1$
knee; the earlier max-over-$\lambda$ statistic is upward-biased and
seed-fragile because the baseline Pareto's tail saturates in $L_0$ --- both
statistics are archived) gives a monotone in-family depth series with tight
3-seed error bars:
\[
  \text{Pythia: } 6L\;\; {+0.0076 \pm 0.0012} \;\to\;
  12L\;\; {+0.0130 \pm 0.0019} \;\to\;
  24L\;\; {+0.0504 \pm 0.0014},
\]
with cross-family 12-layer points GPT-2-small $+0.0175 \pm 0.0025$ and Qwen2
$+0.0413 \pm 0.0025$ (\cref{fig:depthscaling}). These are single points, one
per family: they replicate the gain's \emph{sign}, not its growth with depth,
which is a within-Pythia statement from three points.

Six caveats are part of this figure's claim, and together they are the reason
it is offered as suggestive rather than settled. (i) The Pythia line mixes two
TopK suites (EleutherAI-32k at 70m, MLSAE elsewhere), and the cross-family
points use L1 suites, so dictionary type is not held fixed anywhere in the
figure. The dictionary can move this statistic more than depth does: on
GPT-2-small the knee gap is $+0.0175$ over the public res-jb suite and
$+0.143$ over an L1 dictionary we trained on its cached activations, because
the per-layer baseline is far weaker over the second (held-out $\Rsq$ $0.692$
against $0.844$; the SSM scores $0.834$ and $0.862$). The gap measures the
state against what a per-layer decoder can do with the same codes, so it
belongs to the dictionary as much as to the model. (ii) Within a family, depth is
confounded with width and parameter count (deconfounding via
Pythia-1b/1.4b/2.8b is
recorded future work). (iii) Training-set size also varies along the Pythia
line ($N = 1{,}000 \to 767 \to 1{,}555$ at 6/12/24 layers), and
\cref{sec:evaluation:clt} shows the relative value of cross-layer
parameterisations moving strongly with $N$ in exactly this range (the
SSM$-$CLT gap flips sign); we did not measure the knee gap's own
$N$-dependence, so $N$ stays a live confound for the depth trend.
(iv) The 6-layer point is the Pythia-70m debug lane, whose dataset is built
differently (top-1{,}000 candidates by confidence rather than
threshold-filtered; \cref{app:crossmodel}). (v) The plotted Qwen point is the
12-layer \emph{SAE-coverage prefix of a 24-layer model} --- a
dictionary-controlled contrast (our own L1 SAEs on the same rows at 24 vs 12
layers) gives \textbf{$+0.072$ at full depth vs $+0.043$ on the prefix}, so
public-suite coverage, not depth, limits the plotted point. That $+0.072$ is
an L1-sourced number and \pythia{}'s $+0.050$ is TopK-sourced, so we do not
read the two against each other. (vi) The trend belongs to this statistic
under the LASSO protocol. Under the frozen-source protocol the same contrast,
SSM minus the rank-matched per-layer decoder (\cref{sec:evaluation:clt}), is
not monotone within Pythia: $+0.052$ \CI{$+0.035$}{$+0.069$} at 6 layers,
$+0.022$ \CI{$+0.014$}{$+0.031$} at 12 and $+0.051$ \CI{$+0.048$}{$+0.055$}
at 24 (GPT-2-small $+0.022$, the Qwen2 prefix $+0.037$). There the six-layer
model gains as much as the 24-layer one. The two protocols differ in whether
codes are re-inferred against the target or held fixed, and we have no reason
to prefer either series, so the growth with depth is a property of the knee
gap and not of both measurements.

\paragraph{How much of the gain is learned?}
Running the identical pipeline on a \emph{randomly initialised} \pythia{}
(sources retrained on its own activations) yields a positive but smaller gap:
mean over the $\lambda$ sweep \textbf{$+0.025$ untrained vs $+0.046$ trained}
--- the untrained network carries \textbf{53\%} of the trained gain
(per-$\lambda$ shares averaged; like T4's, a ratio of gaps measured on two
networks, each over its own dictionary, and not a decomposition of the
trained network's gain), and the
learned share \emph{grows with sparsity} (66\% learned at
$\lambda_{L1}{=}10$). The split replicates on GPT-2-small (untrained carries
44\%; both arms there use L1 dictionaries trained on the network's own
activations, so the trained gap is the $+0.143$ of caveat~(i)). These shares
compare gaps at equal $\lambda_{L1}$, not at equal sparsity, and the
difference is not neutral: at a given $\lambda_{L1}$ the untrained network's
SSM runs 1.3--1.8$\times$ denser than its baseline, the trained network's
1.0--1.2$\times$. Interpolating each baseline to the SSM's $L_0$, as for the
knee in \cref{sec:evaluation:gate3}, leaves the trained gaps where they were
($+0.047$, $+0.043$, $+0.041$ at $\lambda_{L1} = 1, 3, 10$) and lowers the
untrained ones to $+0.027$, $+0.020$ and $-0.020$; on GPT-2-small the
untrained share falls from 44\% to 26--42\%. The interpolated statistic has
a bias of its own (\cref{app:prereg}, deviation~5), so we do not replace the
shares with it, but its direction is plain: 53\% is an upper estimate of the
architectural share, and at the sparsest matched point the untrained SSM is
below its baseline. Read against \cref{sec:phase1:pressure}, this is a dissociation
\emph{on the primary model only}: there, AR-on-$\Delta$ \emph{predictability}
is architectural (the untrained network \emph{exceeds} the trained one once
persistence is stripped) while the SSM \emph{reconstruction win} keeps a
$\approx$half learned component --- so the decomposition sees something
training adds that the raw AR statistic does not, concentrated at the sparse
operating points interpretability cares about. On GPT-2-small the two
statistics agree instead (AR learned share $47\%$, reconstruction learned
share $56\%$), so the dissociation is a one-family observation and we do not
claim it more broadly. What carries across both families is the reconstruction
result itself: roughly half the gain is learned, and more than half once
sparsity is matched. At matched parameters the untrained network has no
margin at all (\cref{sec:evaluation:gate3}).

\subsection{Baselines that were not built for
\texorpdfstring{$\Delta$}{Delta}, and it shows}
\label{sec:evaluation:baselines}

The pre-registered indirect baselines both score \emph{below zero} on
update reconstruction: differencing the pretrained per-layer SAE's state
reconstructions ($\hat\Delta_\ell = \hat v_{\ell+1} - \hat v_\ell$) gives
$\Rsq = \mathbf{-0.223}$ even though the same SAE reconstructs the
\emph{states} at $+0.60$ --- the two reconstruction-error terms
$\epsilon_{\ell+1}-\epsilon_\ell$ swamp the small update signal, the bias the
protocol predicted, dramatic at 24 layers --- and the Tuned-Lens-implied
update gives $\mathbf{-0.309}$ (pooled; the layer-averaged version of the same
baseline is the $-0.691$ of \cref{sec:phase1:ridge}). The two
$\Delta$-targeted methods (SSM
$+0.867$, matched per-layer dictionary $+0.816$, both at the sweep's densest
point $\lambda_{L1}{=}0.3$) against the two indirect
ones is the clearest statement of why the update needs to be modelled
directly.

\subsection{Against the unconstrained \texorpdfstring{$O(L^2)$}{O(L2)}
parameterisation (CLT)}
\label{sec:evaluation:clt}

The primary conceptual comparison fits a \emph{residual-write CLT} --- a free
low-rank decoder $W^{\ell'\to\ell}$ at every (source,\,target) pair,
$\hat\Delta_\ell = b_\ell + \sum_{\ell' \le \ell} W^{\ell'\to\ell} u_{\ell'}$
with the same unpenalised per-layer bias the SSM gets, the
unconstrained family our SSM restricts --- on the same frozen sources at the
same per-decoder rank, under the frozen-source protocol. Scope qualifiers
stated once and inherited by every sentence below: residual-write variant (not
MLP-write), frozen shared sources (CLT does not get its own dictionary),
per-pair rank capped at 32--64 (rank 128 exceeds the study GPU;
\cref{app:repro}), $N = 1{,}244$ training examples (1{,}057 where a validation
carve-out is used), and no natively-trained
CLT (limitation). The protocol registered this baseline as two natively
trained variants; the frozen-source residual-write fit is what the study GPU
allows, and the three-axis reading is post hoc (\cref{app:prereg},
deviations~6 and~9). The honest summary is three axes (\cref{fig:clt}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Matched per-decoder rank: a real CLT edge, small at the
    primary rank.} At rank 32
    the best-of-grid gap is SSM$-$CLT $= -0.0142$ \CI{$-0.0185$}{$-0.0099$};
    re-selecting $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$ on a train-internal validation split
    (never test) reproduces it at \textbf{$-0.0090$ \CI{$-0.0136$}{$-0.0045$}}.
    The edge exists at every depth tested --- at the shared grid
    $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}{=}1$ used for all models: 6L $-0.009$; 12L
    $-0.050$ GPT-2, $-0.023$ Pythia-160m; 24L $-0.011$; Qwen2's coarse-grid
    point, $-0.021$, agrees in sign but carries no CI --- and is \emph{not}
    monotone in depth; it tracks model and dictionary. (An earlier apparent tie
    was an artefact of a too-short regularisation grid; we report the corrected
    numbers. Widening the grid moves the primary model's CLT from $0.804$ to
    $0.812$, which is why the depth list above and axis 1's headline differ.)
    Nor is the edge monotone in rank: on the primary model the gap is
    $-0.041$ at rank 16 (on the shorter grid, where every arm's best point
    is the grid edge) and $-0.027$ \CI{$-0.030$}{$-0.025$} at rank 64, so
    ``small'' describes rank 32, not every rank.
  \item \textbf{Matched total parameters: the SSM Pareto-dominates the CLT up
    to 52M, and so does a per-layer decoder of the same size.} Comparing each
    family at its best grid point, SSM rank-64 (16.9M
    parameters, $\Rsq\,0.8155$) beats CLT rank-16 (26.2M, 0.794) \emph{and}
    CLT rank-32 (52.4M, 0.8117); the CLT needs 105M (rank 64, 0.843) to lead
    outright. The rank-32 margin is thin ($+0.004$) and, being a cross-run
    comparison, carries no paired CI --- what the axis supports is that the
    SSM matches or beats the CLT at ${\sim}3\times$ fewer parameters, not that
    it beats it by a lot. Nor is the saving the state's. A per-layer decoder
    with no cross-layer path, widened to the same budgets, does as well: at
    rank 64 (8.41M) it scores $0.8027$, ahead of the rank-32 SSM by $+0.0052$
    \CI{$+0.0031$}{$+0.0075$}, and at rank 128 (16.8M) it scores $0.8131$,
    behind the rank-64 SSM by $0.0024$ \CI{$0.0001$}{$0.0047$}
    (\cref{fig:clt}A). So this axis shows that a free decoder per layer pair
    is the expensive way to buy reconstruction on these sources; it does not
    show that a first-order state is the cheap one.
  \item \textbf{Sample efficiency: on the primary model the CLT edge is
    data-bought.} Subsampling
    the train set flips the sign: at $N{=}300$ the SSM leads by $+0.0204$
    \CI{$+0.0163$}{$+0.0248$}, at $N{=}600$ by $+0.0128$, at $N{=}1{,}244$
    the CLT leads by $0.0105$ (the $N$-series holds one shared regularisation
    grid fixed at every $N$; the extended-grid and validation-selected
    full-$N$ gaps are axis 1's $-0.0142$/$-0.0090$) --- crossover at roughly
    $10^3$ examples for
    52M free parameters versus 8.4M shared ones. The crossover belongs to
    those sizes: we subsampled the primary model only, and on the smaller
    replication models the CLT already leads with 614 (Pythia-160m), 800
    (Pythia-70m) and 1{,}086 (GPT-2-small) training examples (axis 1).
\end{enumerate}

Both cross-layer methods beat the no-propagation per-layer decoder of the
same rank at every depth tested (SSM$-$per-layer $+0.022$ to $+0.052$, the
top of that range shared by the six-layer and the 24-layer model, so under
this protocol the gain is not depth-graded, \cref{sec:evaluation:depth};
CLT$-$per-layer $+0.045$ to $+0.072$; on the primary model
SSM$-$per-layer $+0.0518$ \CI{$+0.0485$}{$+0.0552$} under validation-based
selection). Read together
with H2's failure, the conclusion is symmetric and, we think, useful in both
directions: the free per-pair decoders are \emph{validated} --- they buy a
significant margin on every model, so nothing forces a shared kernel --- and
on the primary model the first-order state-space compression recovers most of
the CLT's gain over the rank-matched per-layer decoder (79--85\%, depending
on the selection protocol) at ${\sim}16\%$ of the parameters, dominating the
CLT at matched budget and in low data. That share is
model-dependent: $85\%$ on Pythia-70m, $64\%$ on Qwen2, about half on
Pythia-160m and $30\%$ on GPT-2-small, so ``most'' is a statement about the
primary model and the six-layer lane, not about every model. It is also a
share of a gain that decoder capacity explains about as well as propagation
does: the rank-64 per-layer decoder of axis~2, at the SSM's parameter count
and with no cross-layer path, recovers 87--98\% of the same CLT gain. Under
this protocol, then, the 79--85\% does not measure what propagation is
worth. The evidence that the state carries something width does not is the
parameter-matched margin under the LASSO protocol
(\cref{sec:evaluation:gate3}) and the structural results of
\cref{sec:evaluation:probe,sec:evaluation:operator}.

\subsection{Stability: refit identifiability follows the dictionary}
\label{sec:evaluation:stability}

Gate 3 recorded one failed clause, and what that clause measures needs
stating exactly. The statistic, match@0.7, refits the SSM from three
different random initialisations over the \emph{same} frozen dictionary and
asks, for each of the top-100 features, whether its \emph{write direction}
--- the feedthrough column $D_\ell[:, j]$, what the SSM learns a unit of
source $j$ writes into $\Dl$ --- has cosine $\ge 0.7$ between refits.
Features are paired by index, because atom $j$ is the same atom in every
refit (\cref{app:stats}); each refit starts from that dictionary's codes
and refines its own copy of them, so ``the same atom'' means the same index
and the same starting activations. What is re-seeded is therefore the SSM, never the
dictionary: the statistic says whether a downstream model's per-feature
readout is pinned down over a given dictionary, not whether independently
trained SAEs agree on the dictionary itself, the question studied by
\citet{paulo2026seeds} and \citet{song2026consistency}. (The protocol's
wording also re-seeded the SAE; with frozen public dictionaries the check
was implemented on the SSM alone, a deviation recorded in
\cref{app:prereg}.) On the public TopK source the statistic was 0.45, below
the pre-registered 0.7, while reconstruction was identical across refits ---
an equivalent-minima degeneracy, unmoved
by $100\times$ stronger operator regularisation and by shrinking $d_z$. Three
post-hoc findings locate it (\cref{fig:stability}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{In the controlled contrast it follows the sparsity
    mechanism.} Training matched L1 and
    TopK dictionaries on the \emph{same} cached activations --- identical
    architecture, matched $L_0$, differing only in the sparsity operator ---
    and refitting the SSM over each: match@0.7 is
    \textbf{1.00 (L1)} versus \textbf{0.08
    (trained TopK)} versus 0.45 (pretrained MLSAE TopK). The reconstruction
    win at the knee is similar but not the same --- $+0.048$ (L1), $+0.038$
    (trained TopK), $+0.049$ (MLSAE) --- and the fit over the trained TopK
    dictionary is the less well-behaved one: at the two sparsest points of
    the sweep it falls below its baseline ($-0.007$ at $\lambda_{L1}{=}10$)
    and then diverges ($\lambda_{L1}{=}30$), where the other two stay ahead.
    The contrast is therefore controlled in its inputs, not in every
    property of the resulting fit. The main analyses rest this on one
    dictionary per mechanism, from one seed; post hoc, we trained two further
    dictionaries per mechanism from different seeds with the same recipe and
    refit the SSM over each: match@0.7 is 1.00 over all three L1
    dictionaries and 0.08, 0.06 and 0.08 over the three TopK ones, so the
    contrast is not a property of one draw (\cref{app:dictseeds}; the
    retrained seed-0 dictionaries and the refits over them reproduce the
    recorded runs exactly). Not a width effect: the two
    trained dictionaries have identical nominal width (8{,}192) and it is the
    \emph{stable} one whose alive set is larger (1{,}178 L1 vs 660 TopK), so
    stability is not bought by narrowness. Cross-model, L1 sources are stable on two further
    families (GPT-2-small 0.95, Qwen2 0.81) while TopK is unstable on two
    Pythia models (0.38, 0.45), with $N$ ruled out by subsampling; the zoo's
    one \emph{stable} TopK point is the six-layer debug lane's EleutherAI
    $k{=}16$ suite (0.99, \cref{app:crossmodel}), so the failure is not
    TopK-universal --- but every TopK source we tested at $\ge$12 layers was
    unstable and every L1 source stable. Retraining
    the L1 dictionary on the train split only (no test leakage; the same
    seed, so a leakage check rather than a second draw) changes
    nothing (match 1.00; knee gap $+0.049$).
  \item \textbf{It is not a gauge artefact.} Gauge-invariant per-feature
    impulse responses (feedthrough plus downstream state path) are as unstable
    (0.29) as raw write directions (0.36 over the gauge analysis's 200-feature
    candidate set; the Gate-3 top-100 statistic quoted above is 0.45), so the
    instability is a genuine non-identifiability of the SSM's
    single-feature readout over redundant TopK atoms --- expected
    for a distributed code where the fit only constrains realised
    combinations.
  \item \textbf{Everything the claims use is stable.} Reconstruction (0.880
    in every refit), the state-operator spectrum (median relative difference of
    the sorted moduli 0.086 across refits --- \emph{not} the sorted-modulus
    cosine of 0.999, which random spectra also achieve;
    \cref{sec:evaluation:operator}), the gauge-invariant state-path decay
    (gap-2 ratio 0.42--0.49 across refits and dictionaries), and
    the feature \emph{set} across data halves (Jaccard 0.89). The top-feature
    write-direction \emph{subspace} is only partly reproduced
    ($\sim$0.6 overlap), and no claim rests on it.
\end{enumerate}

We flag the first finding as independently useful, with its scope stated
plainly. A model fit \emph{downstream} of a TopK dictionary --- here the SSM
--- can assign individual features write directions that a refit from a
different initialisation does not reproduce, so a pipeline that names, steers
or ablates features through such a fit should check that they survive a
refit. Whether the dictionaries themselves are reproducible across training
seeds is a different question, with mixed evidence in the literature
\citep{paulo2026seeds,song2026consistency}. A post-hoc check on our three
seeds per mechanism finds that in this regime they are not, under either
mechanism (\cref{app:dictseeds}): 25\% of the top-100 L1 latents per layer
and 3.5\% of the TopK ones have a counterpart at cosine $\ge 0.7$ in a
dictionary trained from another seed (random and cross-layer nulls: 0\%; no
layer above 0.42 for L1 or 0.15 for TopK). L1 leads only on these high-mass
latents --- over all alive latents the two are level, and under one-to-one
matching (PW-MCC) TopK scores higher --- and 88\% of alive L1 latents still
have cosine $\ge 0.7$ with their random initialisation. The perfect L1
stability above is
therefore a property of the SSM's readouts over a fixed L1 dictionary, not
of the dictionary; dictionary reproducibility cannot be assumed here for
either mechanism. Nor do atoms that never left their initialisation
obviously explain the refit stability: among each layer's 100 highest-mass
latents, fewer L1 than TopK latents stay that close to it (31\% against
56\%, \cref{tab:dictseeds}). We did not test that link directly.

\subsection{The causal existence proof: a clean, fully controlled null}
\label{sec:evaluation:causal}

The pre-registered critical test: find a seed-stable, interpretable feature
(seed-stable in the sense of \cref{sec:evaluation:stability}: its write
direction is reproduced across SSM refits),
subtract its estimated contribution $\delta_\ell$ from the residual stream
during the real forward pass (re-solving the LASSO with the frozen decoder,
so reconstruction error stays untouched), and require --- all four
pre-registered --- a $\ge 0.20$ mean top-1 probability drop on the feature's
top-100 examples, preserved controls, a small matched-random-feature effect,
and a symmetric $\ge 1.0$-nat injection effect on 70\% of silent examples.

\textbf{The test fails, cleanly.} Across the six strongest candidates (from a
pool of 53 seed-stable features with purity 1.00 and legible themes), the
largest ablation effect is \textbf{$+0.024$} --- roughly an eighth of the
0.20 threshold ---
and the injection test returns $-0.02$ nats; controls and magnitude-matched
random features are $\approx 0$, so the splice mechanism is clean. This is
the pre-registered honest landing place, and we report it as such.

Post-hoc diagnostics then locate \emph{why}, with an escalating control
sequence
(\cref{fig:distributed}): amplifying one feature's $\delta_\ell$ ten-fold
drives the answer probability most of the way to zero, saturating at full
removal by $30\times$ (direction correct, natural magnitude small); ablating
each example's top-$k$ \emph{co-firing} features --- pooled over eight
seed-stable candidates, a superset of the six screened above --- crosses
threshold at $k{=}100$ ($+0.237$, CI \CI{0.213}{0.262}, bootstrapped over the
eight candidates, which are the independent unit here, not over test
examples; the per-candidate means span $0.185$--$0.301$, seven of the eight
individually above threshold); a
\emph{count-matched} random group does not ($+0.034$) --- but a
\emph{mass-matched} random group reproduces it ($+0.244$, i.e.\ slightly
\emph{exceeding} the real group), and, decisively, a
\textbf{norm-matched random direction} --- even an isotropic one in
$\mathbb{R}^D$ --- reproduces it too ($+0.224$ isotropic, $+0.204$
contribution-shaped, crossing at the same $k$). Against the isotropic
direction, feature identity retains a significant but ${\sim}5\%$ sliver
($+0.012$ \CI{$+0.004$}{$+0.021$}); against the contribution-shaped direction
$+0.033$ \CI{$+0.020$}{$+0.047$}; against the mass-matched \emph{feature}
group, $-0.008$. We quote the isotropic contrast because it is the control
that isolates direction alone, and note that no magnitude-matched control
leaves feature identity with more than a seventh of the effect.
Off-target specificity of the group ablation is weak and class-dependent: at
the threshold-crossing group size it lowers the example's \emph{own} answer's
log-probability by $2.89$ nats (pooled over candidates) against $2.45$ nats
for a basket of \emph{other} classes' answer tokens (geography features
answer-specific, organization features not). The group effect is therefore \emph{magnitude-driven}: it
demonstrates the query position's generic sensitivity to perturbations of
that size, not a causally privileged code. The lesson has a counterpart at
the level of the dictionary itself: SAE baselines whose feature directions or
activation patterns are constrained to random values match fully trained
SAEs on interpretability, sparse probing and causal editing
\citep{korznikov2026sanity}.

Swapping to the trained L1 dictionary, over which the SSM's readouts are
stable across refits, does not rescue the test: the candidate pool becomes
199/200 seed-stable, the maximum single-feature effect rises nearly five-fold
to $+0.112$, and still no feature approaches 0.20. We do not read the rise as
a cleaner dictionary concentrating causal mass. That dictionary does not
reproduce across training seeds and most of its alive latents sit near their
random initialisation (\cref{app:dictseeds}), so refit-stable is all we can
call it.
\textbf{Factual recall in this model is a distributed, magnitude-driven code
with no single-feature bottleneck under either dictionary we read it with.}
No threshold was adjusted post hoc.

\subsection{Probing the state: reconstruction-trained summaries keep answer
identity}
\label{sec:evaluation:probe}

Does the 32-dimensional state $z_\ell$ carry task information, or is it only
reconstruction machinery? We probe four arms at matched dimension
$d{=}32$ with identical linear probes (hyperparameters selected on a
train-internal validation carve-out): the state; PCA-32 of the current
layer's code $u_\ell$; PCA-32 of the concatenated prefix codes $u_{\le\ell}$
(an information superset of what the state sees); and PCA-32 of the raw
residual $\vl$ as a reference. Target: 205-way answer identity (majority
0.106; permuted-label null at majority).

At mid-depth the state wins against both code summaries on both dictionaries
(\cref{fig:probe}): state$-$code $+0.060$ \CI{$+0.037$}{$+0.084$} (MLSAE) /
$+0.139$ \CI{$+0.105$}{$+0.173$} (L1); state$-$prefix $+0.080$/$+0.173$ at
mid-depth and $+0.110$/$+0.188$ late; and at $\ell{=}17$ --- the
answer-promotion depth where $\kstar$ blips (\cref{fig:kstar}) and per-layer
reconstruction dips (\cref{fig:prefix}, right) --- the state jumps from
$0.57$ to $0.76$, one layer before the code and raw-residual arms, and sits
above the raw-residual PCA there ($0.759$ vs $0.666$). Two features of the
design leave that open to two other readings. The probe's codes are the LASSO
codes, inferred for every example against its \emph{whole} update trajectory
(\cref{sec:decomposition:opt}), and the reconstruction of $\Dl$ reaches the
codes below layer $\ell$ only through $z_\ell$, so whatever those codes pick
up about $\Dl$ and later updates is written along the directions the state
reads: a look-ahead that favours the state over every other arm. And every
competing code arm is a variance-ranked PCA, so the contrast cannot separate
a cross-layer state from any projection of the codes trained to reconstruct
$\Delta$.

Post hoc we checked both, with the statistic and the reading fixed in advance
(\cref{app:checks}). For the look-ahead, the codes were re-inferred for every
depth $\ell$ against $\Delta_0,\dots,\Delta_{\ell-1}$ alone (operators frozen,
everything else as recorded), and the state and the code PCA were rebuilt from
them. With the look-ahead removed the state still beats PCA of the same prefix
codes at mid-depth, by $+0.052$ \CI{$+0.019$}{$+0.085$} (MLSAE) and $+0.133$
\CI{$+0.095$}{$+0.174$} (L1) --- about two thirds and three quarters of the
recorded lead; the rest was look-ahead (state minus causal state $+0.042$,
$+0.045$). The jump at $\ell{=}17$ was look-ahead entirely: across
$\ell = 16, 17, 18$ the causal state reads $0.54$, $0.55$, $0.71$ (MLSAE),
jumping with the raw stream, not ahead of it. For the second reading, the
matched per-layer arm is the feedthrough's own 32-dimensional image of the
code, $D^{(2)\top}_\ell u_\ell$, trained to reconstruct $\Dl$ with no
cross-layer path. The recorded state does not beat it at mid-depth on either
dictionary ($-0.019$ \CI{$-0.038$}{$+0.001$} MLSAE, $-0.013$
\CI{$-0.029$}{$+0.003$} L1), and the causal state falls below the feedthrough
image of the last past code ($-0.024$ \CI{$-0.046$}{$-0.003$}, $-0.041$
\CI{$-0.057$}{$-0.025$}). Since the SSM is linear, the state, the feedthrough
image and the prefix PCA are all 32-dimensional \emph{linear images of the
same codes}: the compressions trained to reconstruct $\Delta$, cross-layer or
per-layer, keep answer-identity information that the variance-optimal
compression discards, and the cross-layer state keeps no more of it than the
per-layer one. Peak decoding reaches 0.775/0.801 top-1 for the recorded state
and 0.736/0.778 for the causal one, from 32 dimensions (majority class 0.106;
with 205 classes over 311 test examples, top-1 is dominated by the frequent
objects, so we read the \emph{contrasts between matched arms}, not the absolute
level). Scope, stated plainly: once the look-ahead is removed the raw stream is
the better readout at every depth band (causal state $-$ raw-residual PCA at
mid-depth $-0.076$ \CI{$-0.101$}{$-0.053$} MLSAE, $-0.024$
\CI{$-0.046$}{$-0.003$} L1; $-0.09$ to $-0.16$ early and late); relation class
is near ceiling for every arm, and there the causal state is below PCA of its
own codes ($-0.049$, $-0.052$ at mid-depth); and model confidence favours the
code arms. What the probe supports is a statement about reconstruction
objectives: specific to \emph{what} is being recalled, at the depths where
recall is assembled.

\subsection{The learned state path attenuates fast}
\label{sec:evaluation:operator}

The state operators are the decomposition's structural readout, and this
analysis of them is post hoc. Getting the
epistemics right here requires a gauge argument we initially got wrong, so we
state it first.

\paragraph{What is and is not a property of the fit.}
\Cref{eq:ssm} has \emph{free per-layer} $A_\ell, B_\ell, C_\ell$. Its
symmetry group is therefore a \emph{per-layer} $T_\ell \in GL(d_z)$,
\[
  A_\ell \mapsto T_{\ell+1} A_\ell T_\ell^{-1}, \quad
  B_\ell \mapsto T_{\ell+1} B_\ell, \quad
  C_\ell \mapsto C_\ell T_\ell^{-1}, \quad
  z_0 \mapsto T_0 z_0,
\]
which leaves every $\hat\Delta_\ell$ unchanged. Only a \emph{global}
$T_\ell \equiv T$ acts on $A_\ell$ by similarity, so \textbf{the eigenvalues of
$A_\ell$ are not gauge-invariant}: even the scalar subgroup $T_\ell = c_\ell I$
rescales $\rho(A_\ell)$ by $c_{\ell+1}/c_\ell$. Applying a random
output-preserving scalar gauge to our fitted checkpoint (predictions unchanged
to $2\times10^{-7}$) moves the median $\rho$ from $0.43$ to $0.26$, the maximum
from $0.88$ to $4.91$, and the count of non-contractive transitions from
$0/23$ to $5/23$. The quantity that \emph{is} invariant --- the $T_\ell$
telescope out of it --- is the realised state path
$C_\ell\,(A_{\ell-1}\cdots A_{j+1})\,B_j$ from source layer $j$ to update
$\ell$. Under the same gauge it is unchanged to $1\times10^{-8}$.

\paragraph{The invariant result.}
The state-path kernel norm \textbf{halves per layer of depth gap}
(\cref{fig:spectrum}, right): normalised
to gap 1, $1 \to \mathbf{0.485} \to 0.211 \to 0.089$ across gaps $1..4$, an
$e$-folding memory of $\tau = \mathbf{1.15}$ layers ($\tau$ is the
least-squares log-linear fit over gaps 1--8; the per-gap ratio tightens from
$0.49$ at gap 2 to ${\sim}0.40$--$0.44$ at every longer gap, which is why the fitted
$\tau$ sits slightly below the $1/\ln 2 \approx 1.44$ that exact halving would
give). This is stable across
three seeds and both dictionaries (gap-2 ratio $0.42$--$0.49$, $\tau =
1.15$--$1.21$ layers). The kernel from a source two layers back has half the
norm of the kernel from one layer back, and from three back a fifth --- so
first-order reads capture essentially all of the state path's weight, and
this is a \emph{mechanism consistent with}
$\kstar\!\approx\!1$. The epistemic caveat still holds: it is estimated on the
same data on which $\kstar$ was measured, so it is an explanation within the
model class, not an independent confirmation; the model-free evidence for
$\kstar\!\approx\!1$ remains the per-depth AR sweeps of
\cref{sec:phase2:kstar}. A second caveat concerns what the decay is a
property of. Every layer has its own sources, and they are codes of the
cumulative state $\vl$, so this fit leaves the state little to carry and a
short memory is what it should show. Withhold the sources and a refit state
carries what it has, how far depending on where the sources stop. In the
prefix sweep of \cref{sec:evaluation:prefix}, in its rerun with the bias
started at the per-depth mean (the recorded sweep's longer tails were partly
its under-fit bias): with sources up to layer~3 the per-layer $\Rsq$ at
$\ell = 4,\dots,7$ is $0.71$, $0.49$, $0.00$, $0.00$; up to layer~7 it is
$0.71$, $0.53$, $0.30$, $0.23$, $0.05$, $0.00$ at $\ell = 8,\dots,13$; up to
layer~11 it is $0.69$ and $0.53$ at the next two layers and stays between
$0.11$ and $0.35$ at every layer after, through $\ell = 23$. From early in
the stack a refit state carries withheld sources two to four layers; from
mid-stack it carries some signal to the end. So $\tau$ describes the path
the full-source fit uses, not a limit on how far the information in the
increments can be carried.

\paragraph{The gauge-dependent result, reported as such.}
In the minimum-Frobenius representative that the $\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}$
penalty selects, the operator is contractive at every layer
(\cref{fig:spectrum}, left; spectral radius
median $0.43$, maximum $0.88$, $0/23$ transitions $\ge 1$; the fit uses
$A_0,\dots,A_{22}$, since the transition out of the last state would feed no
update). That is a reproducible property of \emph{the regularised fit} --- it
recurs across seeds and dictionaries --- and we report it because it is what
one would look at, but no claim in this paper rests on it. We also retire one
statistic we previously quoted: the cross-seed cosine of the sorted modulus
spectrum is $0.999$, but \emph{two independent random spectra score $0.999$ on
the same statistic}, because both vectors are sorted and non-negative. The
honest cross-seed number is the median relative difference of the sorted
moduli, $\mathbf{0.086}$ (and $0.111$ between dictionaries); the per-layer
$\rho$ \emph{profile} correlates only $0.54$ across dictionaries. So the
modulus distribution is reproducible to about $10\%$ and the layer assignment
much less so.

Depth-varying (H2 fails) $+$ a state path that halves per layer $+$ a
${\sim}1$-layer memory is a compact summary of how the fitted model moves
these updates: \emph{information written into the stream persists via the
stream itself, and with a code of the stream at every layer the state path
carries little of it forward.}

\subsection{Where the reconstruction lives: sources at every layer}
\label{sec:evaluation:prefix}

Keeping sources only up to layer $\ell_{\max}$, refitting, and letting the SSM
propagate them forward: held-out $\Rsq$ climbs monotonically over the sampled
cutoffs $\ell_{\max} \in \{0,3,7,11,15,19,23\}$, from $-0.44$
(embedding-only) through
$+0.20$ ($\ell_{\max}{=}11$) and $+0.58$ ($\ell_{\max}{=}19$) to $+0.77$
(full), still gaining $+0.19$ over the last four layers
(\cref{fig:prefix}, left). The embedding-only point is degenerate by
construction --- $v_0$ has no SAE, and this sweep runs under the frozen-source
protocol, which does not re-infer codes, so here $u_0 \equiv 0$ and the state
trajectory is example-independent (under the LASSO protocol $u_0$ is re-inferred
like every other layer's code, \cref{sec:decomposition:sources}) --- and its
$-0.44$ is an optimisation artefact, not a floor. An example-independent
prediction with a free bias can do no worse than the per-depth mean, that
is $\Rsq \approx 0$, and this fit scores $-0.42$ on its own \emph{training}
split. The bias starts at zero, and the sweep's fixed Adam budget does not
carry it to the mean of the final write, whose norm is 2--3$\times$ any
other's: the per-layer $\Rsq$ falls to $-2.4$ at the last depth, and the same
unconverged term sits in the cutoffs $\ell_{\max}{=}3$ and $7$. A post-hoc
rerun that starts the bias at the per-depth mean and is otherwise identical
(\cref{app:checks}) confirms this and replaces the recorded values: the
embedding-only point becomes $-0.003$, and the sweep reads $-0.003$, $+0.07$,
$+0.18$, $+0.40$, $+0.47$, $+0.64$ and $+0.78$ at
$\ell_{\max} = 0, 3, 7, 11, 15, 19, 23$ --- monotone, positive from the first
cutoff that admits sources, and still gaining $+0.14$ over the last four
layers (the recorded $+0.19$ included part of the bias). Late-layer sources
carry reconstruction value that propagation \emph{does not} replace at this
state width. Carried forward, earlier sources recover a per-layer $\Rsq$ of
about $0.7$ at the next layer and $0.5$ at the one after, then between $0$
and $0.35$ (\cref{sec:evaluation:operator}), against $0.81$--$0.90$ once
those layers' own sources are present.
That is what features new at depth would look like, the staged-recall picture
of \citet{geva2023dissecting}; it justifies the design's per-layer sources
and completes a two-sided
ablation ladder with the state ablation: removing propagation costs the
headline-sized margin ($0.77 \to 0.73$ under this protocol --- the state
ablation of \cref{sec:evaluation:gate3} seen from the other side); removing
per-layer sources is catastrophic.
