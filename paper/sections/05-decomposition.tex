\section{The decomposition: a first-order, fully depth-indexed SSM over sparse
sources}
\label{sec:decomposition}

\subsection{Form}
\label{sec:decomposition:form}

Gate 2's decision matrix, applied to \cref{sec:phase2}, fixes the
architecture: pervasive (not smooth, not segmented) depth dependence rules out
shared and interpolated kernels, and $\kstar\!\approx\!1$ makes the depth
latent first-order. The generative model is a linear state-space model with
free per-layer operators:
\begin{equation}
  z_{\ell+1} = A_\ell z_\ell + B_\ell u_\ell, \qquad
  \hat\Delta_\ell = b_\ell + C_\ell z_\ell + D_\ell u_\ell,
  \label{eq:ssm}
\end{equation}
where $u_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{M_\ell}$ are sparse per-layer source codes,
$z_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{d_z}$ is the cross-layer state, $b_\ell$ is an
unpenalised per-layer bias absorbing $\bar\Delta_\ell$, and every operator is
rank-factored ($A_\ell = A^{(1)}_\ell A^{(2)\top}_\ell$, rank $r$; likewise
$B, C, D$). The feedthrough $D_\ell u_\ell$ is the per-layer (no-propagation)
path; the state path $C_\ell z_\ell$ carries whatever earlier layers
contribute. The comparison that isolates the value of cross-layer propagation
is therefore built in: the same model with the state ablated
($\hat\Delta_\ell = b_\ell + D_\ell u_\ell$, \code{use\_state=False}) is a
matched per-layer sparse dictionary sharing the optimiser, sources, sparsity
sweep, and rank. Primary setting: $r = d_z = 32$.

\subsection{Sources}
\label{sec:decomposition:sources}

Source codes initialise from public per-layer SAE latents of $\vl$. On the
primary model these are the 24 \emph{independent single-layer} TopK SAEs
released alongside the MLSAE work for \pythia{}
\citep{lawson2025mlsae} --- one checkpoint per block, not the paper's shared
multi-layer dictionary ($M = 65{,}536$, TopK $k{=}32$, all 24 layers); on the
narrow query-position distribution only 497--4{,}433 latents per layer ever
fire, so each layer is pruned to its alive set ($M^*_\ell \le 4{,}433$) ---
standard dead-latent removal, exact for reconstruction, and legitimate here
because the depth-indexed form permits per-layer widths. $v_0$ has no public
embedding SAE, so $u_0$ is zero-initialised and free. Matched SAE state
reconstruction averages $\Rsq = 0.598$ across layers (two weak public
checkpoints at $v_{18}/v_{19}$, 0.21/0.31, are shared by both comparison arms
and cancel in every gap we report). Because the dictionary turned out to
matter for identifiability (\cref{sec:evaluation:stability}), we also train
matched \textbf{L1} and \textbf{TopK} dictionaries on the same cached
activations --- identical architecture (per-token standardisation, tied
decoder bias, unit-norm decoder columns), differing \emph{only} in the
sparsity mechanism (L1 coefficient 0.5 giving $L_0 \approx 36$, matched to
TopK $k{=}32$) --- including leakage-free variants fit on the train split
only.

\subsection{Optimisation and evaluation protocols}
\label{sec:decomposition:opt}

The loss is squared reconstruction error over depths and examples, plus an L1
penalty on all $u^{(i)}_\ell$ and a Frobenius penalty on the operators
($\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}} = 10^{-4}$). Optimisation alternates exactly as
pre-registered: (i) a proximal-gradient LASSO pass over each example's stacked
codes $(u_0,\dots,u_{L-1})^{(i)}$ with operators frozen --- FISTA with
backtracking \citep{beck2009fista}, validated against \code{sklearn} LASSO
and closed-form least squares, keeping the exact zeros a penalised Adam would
lose --- and (ii) a full-batch Adam \citep{kingma2015adam} sweep over
$\{A_\ell,B_\ell,C_\ell,D_\ell,b_\ell,z_0\}$ with codes frozen, differentiated
through the unrolled SSM. Ten outer iterations; the method was debugged
end-to-end on Pythia-70m-deduped before touching the primary lane.

Held-out numbers in this paper come from two protocols, and conflating them
creates apparent contradictions, so we name them here and tabulate every
operating point in \cref{app:operating}:
\begin{description}
  \item[LASSO protocol (the Gate-3 protocol).] Operators frozen after
    training; test codes re-inferred by the same LASSO against the test
    $\Delta$. This is \emph{reconstruction at matched sparsity} --- the
    standard dictionary-learning evaluation --- not out-of-sample forecasting;
    both comparison arms are treated identically.
  \item[Frozen-source protocol.] Decoders fit by Adam on the \emph{fixed}
    cached codes, no re-inference. Used for the CLT family
    (\cref{sec:evaluation:clt}), where it isolates the decoder
    parameterisation; its absolute levels sit ${\sim}0.08$ below the LASSO
    protocol for every method.
\end{description}

\subsection{Pre-registered sanity checks}
\label{sec:decomposition:sanity}

All mandatory checks pass on the primary fit: no collapse to the bias
solution (operator norms 252 vs bias norm 11; code density $L_0 \approx
0.009$ as a fraction of alive atoms --- source-SAE $L_0$s are quoted as
per-token counts);
per-example reconstruction spot-check median $\Rsq = 0.98$; top-20 features have
mean relation-class purity 0.83 with human-legible themes (e.g.\ an
``employed-by/manufactured-by $\to$ company'' feature at layer 9, a
``premiered-on $\to$ TV network'' feature at layer 1, a ``European place
$\to$ Europe'' feature at layer 22); dataset stability across disjoint halves
top-100 Jaccard 0.89. Seed stability of decoder \emph{directions} failed its
pre-registered bar on the TopK source (0.45 < 0.7) --- recorded at Gate 3 as
a limitation, and subsequently traced to the dictionary type and closed, a
story told in full in \cref{sec:evaluation:stability}.

\textbf{Gate-3 verdict: GO} --- the reconstruction clause is met with margin
(next section), and the then-open seed-stability limitation was scoped to
single-feature decoder identifiability, which no headline claim uses.
